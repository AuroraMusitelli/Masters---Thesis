**📄Master's Thesis Time Series**

This master's thesis analyzes the predictive power of high-dimensional BigVAR models applied to the historical series of the US energy-environmental system over the period 1973–2025. The primary objective was to overcome the over-parameterization limitations of traditional VAR models through the use of regularization techniques based on Lasso penalties and hierarchical lag structures, such as HVAR and VARX-L. 
The methodology employed involves extensive data preprocessing using the Hodrick-Prescott filter with Jumps to manage stationarity and the integration of exogenous macroeconomic variables. The results demonstrate the clear superiority of the regularized models, capable of reducing out-of-sample forecast error by over 70% compared to traditional benchmarks. The analysis of impulse response functions also reveals a strong dependence of CO₂ emissions on fossil fuel consumption and a greater sensitivity of renewable sources to external economic drivers, confirming the effectiveness of high-dimensional approaches for monitoring energy transition dynamics.
