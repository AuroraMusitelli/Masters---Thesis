**📄Master's Thesis Time Series: Evaluating the predictive ability of BigVAR models, an application to energy consumption and CO₂ emissions in the United States**

This master's thesis analyzes the predictive power of high-dimensional vector autoregressive models of the BigVAR framework, applied to energy consumption and carbon dioxide emissions in the United States (1973–2025). The primary objective was to overcome the overparameterization limitations of traditional VAR models through the use of hierarchical regularizations (HVAR) and Lasso penalizations, which were extended to exogenous variables (VARX-L). The applied preprocessing methodology ensures the stationarity of the time series obtained with TRAMO-SEATS and a Hodrick–Prescott with Jumps (HPJ) filter. The results obtained through rolling and expanding window validation show that the penalized models reduce forecast error by 70–85% compared to unstructured benchmarks. Impulse response functions (IRFs) highlight the direct link between fossil fuels and CO2 emissions and the sensitivity of renewable sources to macroeconomic drivers. The effectiveness of the method is confirmed by a bootstrap forecasting procedure, validating the BigVAR procedure as an essential tool for energy transition analysis.
